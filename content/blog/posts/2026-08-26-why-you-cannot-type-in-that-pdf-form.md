A PDF that will not let you type is failing in one of three ways, and they need different fixes. Before you download anything or start hunting for software, work out which one you have. It takes about a minute and it saves you trying the wrong solution twice.

#### Three different problems

The form is a flat scan, the form was never made fillable, or the document is locked. Click where you would expect to type. If nothing at all happens — no cursor, no highlight, no box appearing around a field — you have one of the first two. If you get a message about permissions, or you are asked for a password, you have the third.

Then try to select a printed word on the page. Drag across a heading as though you were going to copy it. If the text highlights, the document has a real text layer underneath. If your cursor draws a rectangle over the page instead, you are looking at a picture of a document.

#### If it is a scan

A scanned page has no text on it in any useful sense. Someone put paper on a glass plate and the result is a photograph. There are no fields because there is nothing there but an image, and no amount of clicking will produce a cursor.

You have two routes. Print it, fill it in by hand, and scan or photograph it back. Or place text on top of the image, where it sits above the picture and prints exactly where you put it. The second is faster, looks tidier, and for the great majority of forms it is accepted without anyone remarking on it.

What it does not do is turn the scan into a real form. The words you add are a new layer, not values in a field. If whoever receives it needs the answers as data — an automated intake, a payroll import — you have to go back and ask for a fillable version.

#### If fields are missing

Plenty of PDFs have a perfectly good text layer and still have no fields, because nobody built any. The document was written in a word processor and exported. It looks like a form because it has lines and boxes drawn on it, but those lines are decoration.

This is the most common case and the least understood one. Typing on top is entirely legitimate here. Nobody receiving the form can tell whether a value was typed into a field or placed over the line, because the printed result is identical either way.

#### If it is locked

Some PDFs carry permission settings that restrict editing, and some carry an actual password. A permissions restriction usually announces itself with a message the moment you try to change something. A password stops you opening the file at all.

Neither is worth working around, and the tools that promise to are not worth the risk. If the sender applied it, ask them to send it again without the restriction, or in a different format. That is a two-line email, it takes them thirty seconds, and it leaves you on solid ground rather than explaining later how you got past it.

#### Making it look right

Whichever route you take, the result should not look pasted on. Match the size of the printed text rather than accepting a default — most form text sits somewhere between nine and eleven points. Use a true black rather than the near-black some editors default to, because the difference shows up when printed next to real ink.

Sit the baseline on the line rather than floating above it, and leave a little room on the left so the first character is not touching the edge of a box. These are small things and they are the difference between a form that looks filled in and one that looks interfered with.

#### Practical notes

Zoom in before you place anything. At fifty percent zoom a line of text can look neatly seated and turn out two millimetres high on paper. Work at a hundred percent or more for anything that has to line up with something printed. Then look at the finished file the way the recipient will, by opening the download rather than trusting the editor view.

[actuallyfreepdfeditor.com](https://actuallyfreepdfeditor.com) opens the file in your browser and lets you place text anywhere on the page, at whatever size and colour the form needs, then hand back a finished PDF. It behaves the same on a scan as on a text document, because in both cases what you add is a new layer sitting on top.
