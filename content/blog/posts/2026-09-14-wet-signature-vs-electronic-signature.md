A wet signature is ink on paper. An electronic signature is a mark attached to a digital document — a typed name, a drawn line, an inserted photograph, or a recorded click. The medium is the entire difference between them, and knowing it does not tell you which one the form in front of you will accept.

#### Ink versus electronic

A wet signature starts with a physical page. You write your name by hand and the mark stays on the paper. Scan that page afterwards and the file contains a picture of a wet signature, but the signing itself happened in ink.

An electronic signature starts with the digital record instead. You type your name, draw with a mouse or a finger, insert a photograph of your handwriting, or click through a service that records the action against the document. Nothing was ever on paper.

#### What live signature means

Live signature is the phrase that causes the most confusion, because it is not a technical category at all. In most workplaces it means a signature made in real time — signing on a screen while somebody watches — rather than dropping in an image you saved months ago.

Different organisations use it to mean different things. Read the instruction as written rather than assuming it maps neatly onto either of the other two terms.

#### Reading the form first

Start with the document's own wording. Look for wet signature, electronic signature, handwritten signature, digital certificate, witness, or notarisation. The appearance of the signature box tells you nothing — a blank line above a printed name looks identical whichever method is required.

If the wording is unclear, ask whoever issued the form. That is a short email and it is the only reliable answer. No article can tell you what your particular form accepts, because it depends on the issuer and on rules that vary from place to place.

#### Placing the mark

Once you know what is wanted, the type of PDF decides the method. If you can click a field and type into it, use the field as intended. If the file is a flat scan, or simply has no fields, place new text over the printed blanks instead — a separate layer that prints where you put it.

Add the signature the same way: draw it, type it, or upload a photograph of one you signed on paper. A drawn or typed mark can be exactly what a workflow asks for. It does not become a certificate-backed signature by virtue of sitting on a PDF.

Then look at the result at full size. Check that the text sits against the right label, that the mark does not cover an instruction, and that the date is correct.

#### Where this stops

A browser editor adds visible layers and little else. It will not edit text already in the PDF, populate real form fields, read a scan, or produce a certificate-based signature, and it will not open a password-protected file.

Covering is not redaction. A white-out block draws over the page; the content underneath remains in the file and can be recovered. Where the hidden thing must genuinely be gone, use a tool that removes it and then check the output by trying to select what you hid.

A visible electronic signature does not, on its own, settle legal effect, identity, or whether a witness was required. The form's wording and the issuing body are where those get answered.

#### Practical notes

If the form asks for a wet signature, the shortest route is still the obvious one: print the page, sign it in ink, and scan or photograph it back. Trying to satisfy a wet-signature instruction electronically because it is more convenient is how forms come back rejected.
